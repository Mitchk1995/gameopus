# Ashford Vale review, 2026-09-29 (critic pass 1)

Reviewed `origin/world-v2` at `630ccc1` ("Bridge deck no longer slows you like wading"): the valley
outside Ashford's walls. The branch moved to `d3a984f` during the review. Nothing under `src/world`,
`public/assets/world` or the vale's content text changed in between (the new commits are villager
looks, combat and scripts), so every finding stands on `d3a984f`.

The town inside its walls was left alone (it is being rebuilt). It was looked at only from outside,
from the roads and gates, and as the place the skyline is seen from.

**Method.** A fresh `npm ci`, `node scripts/bake-world.mjs` (the bake was already up to date) and
`npm run build`, then the built game in Chrome on the real GPU at High quality, driven by scripts
(`scripts/dev/run.mjs` with the `lib.mjs` helpers plus a few of my own). About 400 screenshots from
five kinds of view:

- the player's own camera (the real rig, 3.9 m behind, eye level), every 45 m along all 22 roads;
- the owner's usual view (the rig zoomed out to 9 m and tipped down);
- raised free views 7-9 m up, looking down about 30 degrees, at every site;
- close-ups at 2-3.5 m;
- straight-down plans of the whole vale and eight regions.

Long views: from the square, the spawn and all three gates toward each landmark, and from each
landmark back to town. Numeric checks in Node over `src/world/map.js`:

- a 1 m flood fill on foot from the spawn (slope and water rules as in `scripts/dev/reach.mjs`);
- how level each pad is, and how far it is from water;
- each road's profile against the land (`scripts/dev/roadcheck.mjs`) and its steepest grade;
- signpost boards and distances;
- the height clamp.

I also ran `tests/playtest/world.py`: every check passes ("world ok"). Shots named below are in
`docs/critic/shots-world/`. Where a finding says "(not committed)", the shot is in my session only and
can be retaken from the coordinates given.

**Counts.** 54 findings: 5 "breaks it", 35 "looks wrong", 14 "lacks character".

**What already works, for calibration.**

- **The eastern route.** The east gate to the bridge, the beacon on its knoll, the ramp up into the
  fort, and the gatehouse framed through the fort's east gateway is the first stretch of this game
  that reads as a journey with a destination.
- **The gatehouse** (towers, crenels, the Warden's banners, a portcullis over timber gates) reads as a
  gate from 20 m.
- **The abbey nave, seen from inside.** The tall gable with its lancet and oculus, and the broken side
  walls, make it the best-built structure in the vale.
- **The headframe up close** (braced timber frame, sheave wheel, brick chimney, winding shed) is
  convincing.
- **The field hedgerows** are leafy and irregular: far better than the town's box hedge.
- **Signposts and notices** are well lettered and true to the road graph, and they read with E.
- **The river** has a source and a mouth, and a bridge and a ford across it.

## Big picture: a region, or still a pretty bowl?

**Still a bowl, now with plumbing and signposts.** V2 fixed the valley's logic:

- a river with a source and a mouth;
- roads that go somewhere, with true signposts;
- a real bridge and a ford;
- three ways out that you can walk up to and read;
- one good route (the east).

It did not add a reason to walk anywhere. Every enemy, resource and quest step outside town is
where it was before v2. The new landmarks are scenery with no verb. The eight sites are name boards.
The three ways out are locked, and behind them are dead-end pits. The one thing in every frame, the
wall, is now a sheer, noisy ring with no view out through any pass, so the vale reads as enclosed as
it ever did, arguably more. The owner will leave by any gate, follow a sign to "The Standing Stones" and
find a cairn and a board on a mown lawn.

A region works when three things hold, and today all three fail:

1. From the hub you can see two or three things you want to reach. Today: one, the beacon, and only
   from the east gate.
2. Each direction has something to find and something that stops you for now. Today the stops (the
   three gates) exist, but the finds don't.
3. Every place has a verb: fight, gather, talk, open, climb or read. Today, outside the two camps,
   the mine and the fishing spots, nothing does.

### The mountain rim and the skyline

**M1 (W). The mountain wall is fluted like organ pipes.**
- **Where:** the whole rim. Worst on the north wall above the Highroad (-90,-300), behind the town
  seen from the south gate (-8,104), and behind the lake.
- **Wrong:** Every face is combed into even vertical ribs that run unbroken from the foot to the
  crest. The ribs are all about the same width and fan out like a curtain. DESIGN.md already lists
  these "pleats" as a known problem. The face noise has been reworked since, but the ribs are still
  the first thing you see from every road and gate. The wall reads as a sculpted backdrop, not rock.
- **Instead:** Faces built from a few big masses, not a repeated rib:
  - 3-6 buttresses per 300 m of wall, of very different widths (30-150 m), each broken by 2-3
    ledge bands;
  - gullies that branch, narrow and die out part-way up;
  - talus cones at the gully feet;
  - one or two set pieces a player could name (a leaning pinnacle, an overhanging slab, a cave high
    up).

  No lateral profile of the face should repeat from foot to crest (check 5).
- **Code:** `map.js` `rimWall`. The ribs most likely come from the face displacement `dFace`
  (`fbm(x*0.031…)` and `fbm(x*0.009…)`), which pushes the whole face in and out along the wall, plus
  the ridge noise behind it (`ridged(x*0.0075…)`) and `PEAKS`. The cliff shading in `terrain.js`
  makes them read as flutes. Also `faceAt`.
- **Shots:** `w03_pleats_south_gate.jpg`, `w04_pleats_north_wall.jpg`, `w02_square_no_landmark.jpg`.

**M2 (W). Every cliff turns into zebra stripes at mid distance.**
- **Where:** every steep face 60-300 m from the camera. For example:
  - the mountain behind the beacon, seen from the bridge (100,3);
  - the lake's south cliff, seen from the dock (-52,168);
  - the fort's backdrop (262,70);
  - both gorges.
- **Wrong:** The rock is covered in short bright-white horizontal dashes on near-black, like a zebra
  hide. Commit 06cfa1a eased it off close up, but between 60 and 300 m it is worse than close up:
  - the crag normal is bent harder with distance (up to 2.2);
  - the rock has a specular of 0.55;

  so every small facet that faces the sun flashes white. It turns the whole backdrop into noise and
  makes the vale look dark and dirty.
- **Instead:** At distance, rock should read as big shapes with soft value steps:
  - bend the crag normal at most about 0.5 beyond 60 m;
  - rock specular 0.15-0.2, with roughness of 0.8 or more;
  - strata as broad, low-contrast bands (±10% brightness) that follow the height field's ledges,
    not a per-pixel stripe function.
- **Code:** `terrain.js`, the cliff block (`w[6]`):
  - `tn = normalize(tn - cg * mix(0.7, 2.2, smoothstep(0.03, 0.12, tPx)) * cf)`;
  - `tSpec … + w[6] * 0.55`;
  - `strata` from `bed = tp.y * 0.34 + …`.
- **Shots:** `w05_zebra_beacon.jpg`, `x05_toll_boom.jpg`, `x06_south_gorge_seam.jpg`,
  `f02_fort_inside.jpg`.

**M3 (W). The snow is white paint on the ledges.**
- **Where:** all upper faces. For example, seen from the south gate (-8,104) and the east gate
  (44,15).
- **Wrong:** Snow settles on every gentle tread of the stepped face above about 110 m and on none of
  the risers. It shows as hard-edged white claw marks and blobs in rows, with bare rock between: it
  looks like icing.
- **Instead:**
  - A snow line near 200-240 m that wanders ±25 m.
  - Snow filling couloirs and the gentler upper slopes, with feathered edges and rock breaking
    through.
  - Only a thin dusting on ledges below the line, fading with slope instead of switching on at it.
- **Code:** `terrain.js`
  `wSnow = smoothstep(108.0, 136.0, tp.y + wobble * 34.0) * smoothstep(0.6, 0.34, slope + …)`; the
  snow albedo is in the `w[7]` block.
- **Shots:** `w03_pleats_south_gate.jpg`, `w02_square_no_landmark.jpg`.

**M4 (C). The vale is a pit with no view out.**
- **Where:** everywhere. The foot of the wall is 220-300 m from the square in every direction, and
  its crest is 150-320 m above the valley floor.
- **Wrong:** From every point the horizon is rock. The three pass notches are smooth V-cuts that show
  only sky. Nothing hints at the lands the notices name: the dwarf holds, Redwater Keep three days
  east, Saltmere and the sea. The owner's complaint was "an area surrounded by trees and mountains";
  the new wall answers it with a taller, closer ring of mountains.
- **Instead:** Layered distance:
  - Through each pass, a view of far ranges fading to blue: a low-detail far-terrain ring or
    billboards beyond 400 m.
  - A lower saddle in the south rim, with a bright sea haze beyond it.
  - The silhouette of a keep through the East Pass.
  - One high point you can stand on (the beacon deck, L6) that sees over the wall.
- **Code:** `map.js` `RIM`, `PEAKS`, `rimWall`; the far terrain doesn't exist yet (the terrain stops
  at ±400).
- **Shots:** `w01_spawn_view.jpg`, `w03_pleats_south_gate.jpg`.

**M5 (W). The tallest summit is sheared flat at 320 m.**
- **Where:** the north rim, (-80..80, -400..-360), around the peak at (40,-384); and a small patch in
  the west at (-390,-160). 3,928 m² of the map sits exactly on 320.0 m.
- **Wrong:** `heightAt` ends with `Math.min(h, 320)`, so the tallest peak's top is cut into a level
  white table. It shows plainly in the overhead and the plan, and as a level crest from the north.
- **Instead:** A soft ceiling (compress heights above 280 m with a smooth curve), or lower
  `PEAKS[1]` (150 → 110) so nothing reaches the clamp.
- **Code:** `map.js` `heightAt` (last line); `PEAKS` `[40, -384, 150, 60]`.
- **Shot:** `w07_overhead.jpg` (the white table at top centre).

**M6 (W). The gorges are contour-stepped trenches, and the walls meet the floor in a sawtooth.**
- **Where:** the East Pass gorge (300-360, 85-165) and the South Pass gorge (45-115, 290-350).
- **Wrong:**
  - From above, the gorge walls are concentric terraces. Every ledge is the same height, running
    parallel to the road: a relief model.
  - The floor is a smooth green U-trench.
  - Where wall meets floor, the 1 m terrain grid shows as a zig-zag sawtooth line, and grass tufts
    grow up the rock.
  - The upper walls are extreme zebra (M2).
- **Instead:**
  - Walls with buttresses stepping out into the gorge, a fallen block or two, and a scree fan at
    each gully foot.
  - Ledge heights that vary along the gorge.
  - A floor of gravel and scree, not lawn.
  - A stream on the floor, and no grass on the rock.
  - Smooth the wall's foot (blend over 2-3 m) so no grid triangles show.
- **Code:** `map.js` `gorgeWall` (the ledge rhythm `0.14 * Math.sin(tm * 0.9 + n * 3)`),
  `passNotch`, `gorgeFloorSet`; grass density in `grass.js` (no slope cut-off on rock).
- **Shots:** `x02_east_gorge_deadend.jpg`, `x06_south_gorge_seam.jpg`.

### Roads, the bridge and the signs

**N1 (W). Roads run in trenches and on embankments.**
- **Where.** Cuttings: how deep the road lies below the land it was cut through (from
  `roadcheck.mjs`).

  | Road | Worst cutting | At |
  |---|---|---|
  | Pilgrims' Way | 14.1 m | (0,-160) |
  | Ridge Track | 13.1 m | (207,-200) |
  | Yard Path (a 1.5 m-wide slot) | 11.6 m | (-178,-163) |
  | Highroad | 6.3 m | (-89,-203) |
  | Harbour Road | 6.0 m | (47,288) |
  | Quarry Road | 5.2 m | (-133,-129) |
  | Hermit's Path | 4.9 m | (-252,55) |

  Dykes: how far the finished road stands above the finished ground 5 m out on *both* sides.
  `roadcheck.mjs` doesn't see these, because it compares against the land before the water is
  carved.

  | Road | Height above both sides | Where |
  |---|---|---|
  | Stones Path | 1.3-3.1 m for 60 m | (67..73, -26..-86), across the heath |
  | Shoal Path | 1.3 m, rising to 5.6 m | (-132,181) to the pad |
  | Lighthouse Path | 2-5 m | the whole 19 m |
  | Harbour Road | about 2.2 m | (22,201) and (-32,167) |
  | Waystation Spur | 1.0-1.2 m | its whole 12 m |
- **Wrong:** Each road's profile is taken from `surface0`: the land with its pads, *before* the river
  and lake carve their valleys and shores. It is smoothed over about 30 m and clamped to its grade,
  then stamped in with 1.2-1.5:1 banks. So:
  - tracks on slopes run in smooth grass V-trenches with a sharp crease along each lip;
  - every road near water rides a railway embankment above the lowered floodplain, marsh or shore.

  No footpath does either; a path follows the ground and is steep where the ground is steep.
- **Instead:**
  - Profile roads on the finished land (after the river and lake carve, before roads).
  - Smooth over 8 m or less, and clamp grades only on the `road` class.
  - Tracks and paths may reach 25-35%, with steps where needed.
  - Cut at most 2 m. Where more is needed, re-route with a switchback, or make it a short rock
    cutting (vertical face, rock texture).
  - Fill at most 0.8 m, except at the bridge and ford.
- **Code:** `map.js` `buildProfile` (`W = 7` smoothing, the grade clamp; `surf = surface0`),
  `computeProfiles`, `CLASS` (`cut`, `fill`, `infl`), `roadStamp`, and the order of operations in
  `heightAt`.
- **Shots:** `r01_pilgrims_way_cutting.jpg`, `r02_quarry_pad_pit.jpg` (the Ridge Track's cut);
  `r_headframe_040`, `c_stones_arrive` and `b_marsh2` (not committed).

**N2 (W). The bridge floats on a lawn.**

## Recurring mistakes, and a check for each

As with the town, the automated checks pass on all of the above. I ran `world.py` (reachability,
sealed closures, notices, signposts), and every check passed. These checks ask whether things
*exist* and are *reachable*. They never ask whether things sit right on the land, make sense where
they are, or can be seen. Each check below reads data that already exists: `map.js` (pads, roads,
profiles, `JUNCTIONS`, `LANDMARKS`), the colliders, the SiteKit and Batcher logs, and rendered
frames. Put them in `world.py` or `look.py` as FAIL lines.

1. **Built things that don't meet the ground or each other.** V5 (the jetty in the mound), N2 (the
   bridge and the road), L3 (the berm hiding the abbey), L4 (the headframe on a rock blob).
   - **Measure:** For every collider flagged `floor` that belongs to a built structure (SiteKit,
     `resources.js`, the Batchers), sample a 0.5 m grid under its top. Fail if `heightAt` rises above
     the top by more than 0.05 m anywhere (a deck can't be underground), unless the shape is tagged
     `abutment`.
   - For each structure a road runs onto (the bridge, gates, closures), fail unless the road's paint
     weight (`groundAt(x, z)[0]`) is at least 0.5 within 0.5 m of each end.
2. **Sites placed where their story can't happen.** P1-P5, F3.
   - **Measure:** Add `needs` to every `POIS` row, for example:
     - `hamlet: { water: 8, above: 2 }`;
     - `sawmill: { river: 6, above: 1.5 }`;
     - `sunken: { depth: [0.2, 1.0] }`;
     - `shrine: { knoll: 3 }`;
     - `quarry: { face: 8 }`.

     `world.py` computes, for each pad: distance to water and height above it, depth range over the
     pad, and relief within `r + 15` m, then checks them against the row.
   - Every pad must also be at least 90% level (within 0.3 m of its level) and at least 90%
     reachable (the flood fill).
   - The same rule for camps: `GOBLIN_CAMP` must have no line of sight to any town gate.
3. **Roads that don't sit on the land.** N1, N7.
   - **Measure:** Promote `scripts/dev/roadcheck.mjs` into `world.py`, comparing against the
     *finished* `heightAt` rather than `surface0` (the old comparison is why the dykes by the water
     went unseen). Per road class, fail if:
     - the cut (land minus road within `infl`) exceeds 2.5 m for paths and tracks, or 4 m for roads;
     - the finished road stands more than 1.2 m above the finished ground 5 m out on *both* sides,
       outside the bridge and ford;
     - the grade exceeds the class's grade × 1.1 anywhere, ford ramps and pinned ends included.
4. **Views that nobody checked from the player's spot.** L1, L2, M4.
   - **Measure:** In `look.py`, render from fixed viewpoints: the square's centre and corners, the
     spawn, each gate. Use an ID pass: each landmark in its own flat colour, everything else black.
   - Fail if a landmark listed for that spot (a new `LANDMARKS[k].from` list in `map.js`) covers
     fewer than 600 px at 1280 × 720.
   - Also fail if the luminance contrast between the landmark and a 20 px band around it, in the
     normal render, is below 0.2.
5. **Surface shading that turns into patterns.** M1 (pleats), M2 (zebra), M3 (snow bands), V2
   (sunburst), V3 (dashed foam).
   - **Pleats, from the height field:** Along each 300 m stretch of the rim, take the across-wall
     profile of `heightAt` at 10 m and at 60 m into the wall (sampled every 2 m, detrended). Fail if
     the two correlate above 0.6: the same ribs from foot to crest.
   - **Zebra, from the render:** In `look.py`, render six fixed rim views plus a steep-terrain mask
     pass. Over the masked pixels, fail if more than 8% differ in luminance by over 0.45 from the
     pixel 3 px above or below.
   - **Foam:** In a still-water view of the lake shore, fail if foam pixels cover more than 40% of
     the shoreline pixels.
6. **Placeholder primitives and near-black materials.** A1 (cabbage balls), A2 (wheat), H1
   (heather), F2 (tents), F4 (ore potatoes), X4 (cube lanterns).
   - **Measure:** A unit check over `plants.js` kinds: every vertex colour times its `lum` factor
     must be at least 0.06 linear luminance.
   - No plant kind may use an `IcosahedronGeometry` of detail 1 or less, or an untextured
     `CircleGeometry`.
   - Extend the town review's check 5 (untextured area over 0.3 m² per labelled object) to
     `fight.js` tents and `resources.js` rocks, and fail any emissive box under 0.5 m that isn't
     inside a lantern frame.
7. **Promises the world doesn't keep.** P5 (no stones), A4 (no farm), X5 (the notices name people
   who say nothing), N5 (two quarries), L6.
   - **Measure:** A manifest of every place name used on a board or in text (`ends`, `POIS` names,
     `EXITS` notice text).
   - Each name must map to exactly one location.
   - After wave 2, each must have a tagged built object within 20 m other than its own signs.
   - Every NPC named in a notice must have a dialogue line containing one of the notice's key nouns
     (Guild, Warden, seal…).
8. **Dead ends and fakes behind closures.** X1.
   - **Measure:** For each exit, fail if the road beyond the closure is longer than 20 m.
   - With the closure unlocked (`setLocked(id, false)`), no cell beyond it may be reachable unless
     that exit is flagged as leading somewhere.
9. **Signs that don't help.** N3, N4.
   - **Measure:** In `world.py`, fail a board for an end nearer than 60 m, and fail two posts within
     30 m of each other.
   - Warn when one post carries more than four boards.
10. **Hard clamps.** M5.
    - **Measure:** Count `heightAt` samples on a 2 m grid that equal the clamp value. The count must
      be 0.
11. **Carried-over findings.** F2, F4 and V6 are the town review's O4, O3 and O2, still open.
    - **Measure:** Keep critic findings in a tracked list, each with a test id or a "won't fix" note.
      CI prints the open ones.
