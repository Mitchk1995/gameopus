# Ashford town review, 2026-09-29 (critic pass 1)

Reviewed `origin/main` at `3b3cd1b`, then re-confirmed every finding on `982f95a`. Main has
since moved to `93e0aa1`, but nothing under `src/world` or `src/content` changed in between (only
combat, UI and animation code), so the whole list still stands. The in-flight `vale-*` branches
(a bridge, leafy hedgerows, new cabbages) are not on main and were not judged.

**Method.** A built game in headless Chrome on the real GPU, at High quality. About 260 screenshots
from four kinds of view: the player's own camera at eye level (follow distance 3.9 m); the owner's
usual view (the real rig zoomed out to 9 m and tipped down 30 degrees); raised free views 5 to 9 m
up; and close-ups at 2 to 4 m. I also ran a plan audit over `src/world/ashford.js`: every
building, door, wall run, plot, prop and gate, checked against every other one. A reachability
flood over the collider grid, using the helpers in `tests/playtest/town_helpers.js`, backed that
up. Shots named below are in `docs/critic/shots/`. Where a finding says "(not committed)", the
shot is in my session only and can be re-taken from the coordinates given.

**Counts.** 46 findings: 5 "breaks it", 31 "looks wrong", 10 "lacks character".

**What already works, for calibration.** The kiln (brick courses, iron bands, arched stoking
mouth) is the one object that reads as designed. The cobbles are good. The stall goods sit
properly on their counters. Lake Street is as tight as a real street should be.

## Top 10, most damaging first

1. **O1. No bridge.** Bridge Street's road runs into 2.1 m of water at x 84 to 98 and carries on
   up the far bank. The player stops at x 84, so nothing east of the river can be reached: the
   big farm, the eastern woods, the bandit camp, and the wreck at x≈196 plus the bandit captain,
   which are steps 2 and 3 of the Ledger quest. The lore and Garrow both promise "the stone
   bridge".
2. **P1. NPCs are clones, and some wear the player's clothes.** Garrow wears the player's exact
   outfit, hood, hair and face. Old Tam is the player with a beard. Ysolde is the female cut of
   the same green ranger outfit. Aldwyn and Brom are identical twins; so are Bess and Wenna.
   Everyone, the player included, has the same silver hair.
3. **B1. Grass grows through every floor.** It comes up through the planks of the bank, the store
   and the inn. The smithy and the potter's workshop have no floor at all: raw terrain and grass.
4. **Boundaries run through things** (E1, E2, E4, G2, G8, B5, S2, S3):
   - the town wall runs through the quarry garden;
   - the south-west hedge runs through garden c, with a washing-line pole inside the hedge;
   - the south-east hedge cuts a haystack in half;
   - a woodpile is pushed through a fence corner;
   - hay bales pass through the hay wagon's wheel;
   - graves sink into the chapel wall;
   - a signpost stands flat against a house;
   - a lamp post stands inside the smithy's trough.

   The owner reported this whole class of mistake, and every example of it is still there.
5. **G1. Two back gardens are sealed.** Gardens a and b (a washing line, a woodpile) have their
   gates in a 0.4 m slot against the cottage's back wall. The cottages have no back doors. Not
   one cell inside either garden can be reached on foot (0 of 62, 0 of 50).
6. **B2 and B3. The chapel precinct.** The chapel and its tower are two ordinary houses. The
   churchyard's low wall runs straight across both front doors, and the lych-gate is stuck on the
   chapel door like a porch. This is the owner's "low stone wall built in front of two houses'
   front doors" and "chunky, odd porch".
7. **B6, B7, B13. Every building is the same house.** The bank, store, inn, barn, both stables,
   the toll house and the watch house are the cottage kit with a different label. They have
   glazed arched windows, house doors, no signs and no shopfronts, and every roof is the same
   orange tile. There is no landmark, and only the minimap says what any building is.
8. **G3 to G7. Placeholder plants and hay.** The beans are green traffic cones on sticks. The
   cabbages are smooth green balls (melons). The "herbs" are candy-coloured lollipops. The beds
   are flat brown boxes with grass growing through them. The haystacks are 12-sided cylinders
   with cones on top.
9. **E5 to E8. The town edge's joinery.**
   - The hedge is a smooth green box with stamped ivy decals.
   - At every corner, two wall piers overlap into an eight-pointed star.
   - At the north and east gates, the wall's end pier is built into the gate pier, at a
     different height.
   - The gap posts are buried in the ends of the hedge.
10. **S1, B9, B10, B12. Sloppy work in the market square.**
    - The well roof is still one flat four-tile slab.
    - The potter's pots, and a metal pan, sit on the cobbles.
    - The forge fire is a flat orange sticker.
    - Iron lanterns hang at face height beside every door.

## Findings

Severity: **B** = breaks it, **W** = looks wrong, **C** = lacks character. Coordinates are world

### Town edge: wall, hedge, gates

**E1 (B). The town wall runs through the quarry garden.**
- **Where:** the quarry garden, x -50..-38, z -22..-8; the north-west diagonal wall, (-54,-8) to (-40,-28).
- **Wrong:** The wall line cuts across the plot, leaving its corner (-50,-22) 4.8 m outside town.
  Bean rows and the fence carry on under and beyond the wall. This is the owner's "town wall
  through an allotment fence".
- **Instead:** Every plot corner should be at least 1.5 m inside the wall's inner face. Here that
  means `20x + 14z ≥ -1138` at each corner. Shrink the garden to x -44..-38, z -16..-8, or drop
  it: there are already three allotments.
- **Code:** `ashford.js` `PLOTS` 'quarry garden'; `OUTLINE` edge 0.
- **Shots:** `e05_quarry_garden_raised.jpg`, `e06_quarry_garden_wall_close.jpg`.

**E2 (B). The south-west hedge runs through cottage garden c.**
- **Where:** garden c, x -49..-43.5, z 57..62; the hedge, (-44,64) to (-54,54).
- **Wrong:** The corner (-49,62) is 2.1 m outside town, and a fence corner stands on the far side
  of the hedge. The washing line's west pole, at (-48.4,59), is inside the hedge, with the cloths
  pressed flat against it.
- **Instead:** Keep every corner at least 1 m inside the hedge's inner face, which means
  `x − z ≥ −105.7`. That leaves garden c too small to matter, so delete it or move it.
- **Code:** `ashford.js` `PLOTS` 'cottage garden c'; the washing line in `PROPS`.
- **Shot:** `f11_garden_c_outside.jpg`.

**E3 (W). The kitchen garden's corner sits on the north-east wall.**
- **Where:** the kitchen garden, x 22..34, z -8..-1; its corner (34,-8) is on the line
  (14,-28) to (38,-4).
- **Wrong:** The fence rails run into the wall body and its pier, and the wall and pier caps
  overlap at different heights.
- **Instead:** Pull the plot back to x 22..32.5, z -6..-1, which keeps `x − z ≤ 38.9` and so
  2.2 m clear of the wall's centreline.
- **Code:** `ashford.js` `PLOTS`.
- **Shot:** `g12_kitchen_garden_wall.jpg`.

**E4 (W). The south-east hedge cuts the farmyard haystack in half.**
- **Where:** haystacks at (31.5,58.5), r 1.7, and (33.5,54.0), r 1.3; the hedge, (38,52) to (26,64).
- **Wrong:** Half of the big stack is outside town, and the small one touches the hedge.
- **Instead:** Keep the whole footprint at least 0.5 m clear of the hedge, which means
  `x + z ≤ 90 − 1.414 × (0.63 + r + 0.5)`. For example, put the stacks at (29.5,55.5) and
  (32.5,52.5).
- **Code:** `ashford.js` `put('haystack', …)`.
- **Shot:** `h06b_haystack_hedge_out.jpg`.

**E5 (W). Two piers overlap into a star at every corner.**
- **Where:** all eight outline corners, e.g. (38,-4) and (-40,-28).
- **Wrong:** `stoneWall` puts a pier at both ends of every run, each turned to its own run, so the
  two runs' end piers overlap at 45° into an eight-pointed star cap. The runs' caps butt into it
  at different heights.
- **Instead:** Place one square corner pier per vertex, turned to the bisector, and don't emit end
  piers where two runs meet.
- **Code:** `townkit.js` `stoneWall` (the pier loop over `i = 0..n`); `village.js` `#edge`.
- **Shot:** `w01_corner_ne.jpg`.

**E6 (W). At the gates, the wall's end pier is built into the gate pier.**
- **Where:** the north gate (-27,-28) and the east gate (38,15).
- **Wrong:** The wall is cut at ±2.8 m and ends in a pier 0.85 m wide and 1.6 m high. The gate
  piers stand at ±3.35 m and are 1.15 m wide and 3.1 m high. The two overlap by 0.45 m, the
  lower pier sticks 0.4 m into the gateway, and their caps sit at different heights. This is the
  owner's "wall caps and piers overlapping at different heights". At the south gate, the hedge
  pokes about 0.1 m past the gate pier's face.
- **Instead:** Cut the wall and hedge at the gate pier's outer face (±3.9 m), with no wall pier
  there. The gate pier is the wall's end.
- **Code:** `ashford.js` `buildWalls` (`o.width / 2`); `townkit.js` `gate` (`half = width/2 + 0.55`).
- **Shot:** `e03_tollhouse_eye.jpg` (right edge).

**E7 (W). The gap posts are buried in the hedge ends.**
- **Where:** the garden gap (-54,47) and the farm gap (20,64).
- **Wrong:** Posts 0.3 m square and 1.9 m tall, with little pyramid caps, stand inside the
  1.25 m-thick hedge ends and show only above the top, like birdhouses.
- **Instead:** A field gate: two round oak posts 0.2 m thick standing just outside rounded hedge
  ends, with a 1.2 m five-bar gate hung open on one of them.
- **Code:** `village.js` `#edge` (the `GAPS` loop).
- **Shots:** `h06b_haystack_hedge_out.jpg` (left); `f11_garden_c_outside.jpg` (top left).

**E8 (W). The hedge is a green box with ivy stickers.**
- **Where:** the whole south and west edge, and every hedge run.
- **Wrong:** It has smooth flat sides, a flat top and straight vertical cut ends. It is the same
  thickness everywhere, and the `MI_Vine` ivy decal is stamped over it in columns. It reads as a
  painted board, not a hedge. (The owner reported this.)
- **Instead:** A dense leafy mass:
  - an irregular dark core under two or three layers of alpha-tested leaf cards (hawthorn or
    beech);
  - an outline that wanders ±10 to 15 cm, and a height that varies from 1.5 to 2.0 m along the run;
  - darker gaps showing through, and woody stems and grass tufts at the foot;
  - rounded ends.

  If `vale-nature`'s "leafy hedgerows" does this, use it for the town too.
- **Code:** `townkit.js` `hedge` and `hedgeBlock`.
- **Shot:** `a05_hedge_close.jpg`.

**E9 (C). The town wall and gates are garden fittings.**
- **Where:** all gates, and the north and east walls.
- **Wrong:**
  - The wall is 1.25 m high with a slab cap and a pier every 5.5 m: a suburban garden wall.
  - The gates are two piers, a plank beam and a board, with no gate leaves.
  - The gate "lanterns" are bare glowing boxes stuck to the pier faces. They read as lit windows.
- **Instead:** If the low wall is intended (DESIGN.md says "low stone wall"), make it a dry-stone

- **Code:** `townkit.js` `stoneWall` and `gate`.
- **Shot:** `e03_tollhouse_eye.jpg`; south gate `b03` (not committed).

### Buildings

**B1 (B). Grass grows through every floor.**
- **Where:** the bank (-8,-2), store (-28,10), inn (14,7), smithy (11,23) and potter's workshop (-28,22.5).
- **Wrong:** Dense tufts come up through the plank floors of the bank, store and inn. The smithy

- **Instead:** No grass may draw inside any building footprint (plus 0.3 m). Paint the footprints

  `footprint(b, 0.3)`, then re-bake). Give the open workshops real floors: flagstones in the
  smithy, beaten earth strewn with straw, or boards, in the potter's.

**B2 (W). The chapel and bell tower are two houses.**
- **Where:** the chapel (1.8,-19.6) and the tower (7.6,-18.6).
- **Wrong:** Both have house doors with glazed lights, wall lanterns, shutters, arched cottage

**B3 (W). The churchyard wall runs across both front doors.**

## Recurring mistakes, and a check for each

Every automated check passed on all of the above. What they shared: they check the plan's
*intent* (a door "faces a street", a prop is "near something"), not what is actually *built*
and how it *meets* its neighbours. Each check below reads data that already exists at runtime:
`world.village.layout` (the `ashford.js` plan), `world.colliders` (every solid shape), the
Batcher log on `village.mesh.userData.pieces` (every kit part and every labelled procedural
piece, with its world matrix and geometry), and `colliders.buildings` (every `buildHouse`
result). Put them in `town.py` or `geometry.py` (both are `# ci`) as FAIL lines.

1. **Things crossing boundaries.** Walls, hedges and fences run through plots, props, and each
   other: E1, E2, E3, E4, G2, G8, B5, S2, S3.
   - **Measure:** In `TownKit.begin`, tag every collider shape it creates with an owner id (and
     tag `addSolids` shapes with the kit prop). Then, for every pair of solid shapes with
     different owners, fail if they overlap in plan by more than 0.05 m and their height spans
     overlap. Allow pairs that are meant to touch (a bale stacked on a bale, goods on a counter).
   - **Plan-level twin:** every `PLOTS` corner must satisfy
     `polyDistance(x, z) ≤ −(wall half-thickness + 1.5)`. No prop footprint (`PROPS` plus a radius
     table) may come within 0.3 m of any `WALLS` segment, plot side or building footprint, unless
     it is an allowed wall-mounted type.
2. **Places you can't get into.** G1 (sealed gardens). The existing test only checks doors, and
   `doorways()` only knows front doors.
   - **Measure:** Run the existing spawn flood fill. For every plot, at least 80% of the free
     cells inside must be reachable, and so must the point 1.2 m outside its gate. Any plot side
     within 2 m of a house wall must have a door in that wall that opens into the plot. Extend
     `doorways()` to every side.
3. **Barriers in front of doors.** B3 (the churchyard wall), E6 (gateways narrowed).
   - **Measure:** For every doorway, sweep a 1.2 x 2 m rectangle outward from the door. It must be
     collider-free and must touch a street, the square or a yard. Any wall, hedge or fence segment
     within 4 m in front of a door must have an opening at least 1.2 m wide within 1 m of the
     door's axis. For gates, the clear width between colliders must be at least 0.9 × `width`.
4. **Grass or terrain where a floor should be.** B1, G6.
   - **Measure:** For every building footprint (inset 0.3 m) and every crop bed, sample 25 points
     and read the ground weights (`groundAt` / `townGround`, which is what the grass shader
     uses). Fail if the meadow weight is above 0.05. Then raycast down from 1.5 m above the floor
     level: the first hit must be a mesh whose material is a floor material (`Floor_*` or a
     flagstone material), never the terrain.
5. **Placeholder primitives in for real objects.** G3 to G7, G9, S1, O3, O4.
   - **Measure:** From the Batcher log (`proc` entries carry the geometry and material) and the
     tagged scene objects, compute each labelled object's untextured surface area: the triangles
     whose material has no `map`, excluding glass, emissive and water. Fail any object with more
     than 0.3 m² of it. Also fail any `CylinderGeometry` with 12 or fewer radial segments and
     `radiusTop < 0.15 × radiusBottom` (a cone) or `IcosahedronGeometry` of detail 1 or less,
     unless listed in an allowlist with a reason. That catches the bean cones, cabbage and flower
     balls, the haystack cones, the well roof, soil boxes, logs, cloth cards and tents.
6. **One building doing another's job.** B2, B6, B7.
   - **Measure:** In `ashford.js`, give each role a required and a forbidden feature list and
     assert it against `colliders.buildings[i].parts`. For example:
     - **barn:** no `Window_*` parts; a door opening at least 2.5 m wide.
     - **chapel:** no `Lantern_Wall` or shutters; a tower within 0.2 m, and at least three lancet
       parts.
     - **public ids:** a part with role 'sign'.

     A missing builder then fails loudly, instead of falling back to a cottage.
7. **Joins between repeated pieces.** E5 (star piers), E6 (doubled piers), E7 (posts in hedges),
   B8 (missing corner posts).
   - **Measure:** From the log's labelled pieces ('wall pier', 'gate', 'gap post', 'hedge'),
     fail if any two pier boxes overlap in plan. Fail if a wall pier stands within 1.0 m of a gate
     pier. Fail if a post's footprint lies inside a hedge's box. Adjacent cap tops within 0.3 m
     of each other in plan must match in height to 0.02 m. For `buildHouse`, a closed corner (both
     adjacent walls present) must have a corner part.
8. **The same NPC twice, or the player's outfit on an NPC.** P1.
   - **Measure:** In a unit check over `src/content/people.js` and `main.js`, key each look as
     `outfit | hair | beard | tint`. Fail if a key is repeated, or if any NPC uses the player's
     outfit id.
9. **Things mounted at head height.** B12.
   - **Measure:** For every kit part matching `WALLMOUNT` in the log that projects more than 0.2 m
     from its wall and faces a street, square or doorway, the bottom of its bounds must be at least
     2.1 m above the ground.
10. **Texture scale and trim-sheet misuse.** S1, S6, G7.
    - **Measure:** Extend the geometry audit's texel-density check (`TOL.density`, `TOL.stretch`)
      to procedural pieces, not only kit parts. Fail trim-sheet materials (`MI_WoodTrim`,
      `MI_Trim_Metal`) on any geometry thinner than 0.15 m in two axes. Fail any tiled material
      showing fewer than 3 repeats across a surface over 1 m² (the four-tile well roof).
11. **Promised but missing.** O1 (the bridge the lore describes), O2 (the dock's crates), and the
    inn's "Crooked Pike" sign.
    - **Measure:** Keep a manifest of named landmarks in the plan (`bridge`, `dock crates`,
      `inn sign`, ...). Each must exist as a tagged object and be reachable. Add a CI walk: spawn
      at (70,1), hold W eastward for 10 s, and fail unless x > 100. Add another walking the Ledger
      quest's goals from the gate.
12. **Dead space.** S7, G11. Warn, don't fail.
    - **Measure:** Over the town flood grid, compute each free cell's distance to the nearest
      building, prop, plot, street or wall. Report any connected region of cells more than 6 m
      from everything with an area over 60 m², giving its centre, so a designer decides what goes
      there.
